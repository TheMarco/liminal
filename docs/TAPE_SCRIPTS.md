# The ten elevator recordings — segmented scripts

Written against `STORY.md` (2026-08-04 revision). Every tape: Dr. Evelyn
Cross sits at a table in the same room, facing a fixed camera, fluorescent
light. Each segment is 15 seconds. Every Says line is 34-42 words, which
fills 15 seconds at a measured delivery with only short natural beats.

Acting rule for all ten tapes: Cross is controlled, and the horror is the
control failing in small ways. She never screams, sobs, or performs fear.
The creepy material is behavioral: pauses a half-second too long, glances at
something off frame she never explains, smiles where a smile doesn't belong,
stops mid-word and resumes as if nothing happened, holds eye contact past
the point of comfort. One such moment per segment, never more.

---

## Tape 1 — Casino floor — "The First Elevator" (60s)

*Performance: brisk, professional, almost pleasant. The only wrong note is
at the very end. Viewers should feel the wrongness in hindsight, not now.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "Recording one. Friday, March the fourteenth. Eleven forty at night, if my watch is right, and it always is. Dr. Evelyn Cross, for the record. Though I don't know yet who the record is for."
Does: Sits upright, squares the notebook, checks her watch with a small confident smile. On the last sentence the smile fades a fraction early, before the sentence ends.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "I took the service elevator down. It ran far longer than any basement should allow. When the doors opened there was floor space. Carpet, lighting, power. Everything running, everything maintained, and no one anywhere. I called out twice. Nothing answered."
Does: Delivers it like a field report, hands calm on the notebook. On "nothing answered" she stops, listens for one second to the room around her, then continues as if she hadn't.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "The simple explanation is unmapped construction. Old foundations, sealed levels. Cities bury things all the time. Except I walked twenty minutes in a straight line without reaching an outer wall, and the building above me is eighty metres wide. I measured it."
Does: Taps the notebook once on "except," pace quickening slightly, a scientist enjoying a puzzle. "I measured it" lands flat and cold, the enjoyment suddenly gone.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "So the simple explanation is wrong, and I don't have a better one yet. That's fine. Most explanations start wrong. I'm going to document everything I can verify. If someone finds these recordings... start where I started."
Does: Closes the notebook briskly. On "if someone finds these" her eyes flick to the left of the lens, at something off frame, and stay there while she finishes the line. Then she reaches toward the camera without looking back at it.

## Tape 2 — Mall floor — "Places Without People" (60s)

*Performance: still professional, but the certainty has a crack in it. She
keeps catching herself listening.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "Recording six. I think six. The sixth one worth keeping, anyway. Saturday the fifteenth. Or it was the fifteenth when I last checked, and I'll admit that check was longer ago than it should have been."
Does: Starts confident, then stumbles on the number and covers it with a small dry laugh that stops abruptly, like she heard how it sounded.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "Everything here still performs. Lights cycle on a schedule. Music plays to no one, on a loop I've started to recognize. This morning a machine dispensed change at me, exact change, and there was nobody anywhere to have paid it."
Does: Leans in slightly, genuinely fascinated. On "exact change" she holds up two fingers as if presenting evidence, and her hand has a faint tremor she doesn't seem aware of.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "My first thought was abandonment. People left in a hurry, and the systems kept going without them. It's a comfortable theory. But abandoned places decay. Dust, damp, failure. Nothing here is decaying. Nothing here has ever been allowed to."
Does: Looks down at her pages. On "allowed to" she goes still and her eyes come back up to the lens slowly, arriving before her head does.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "It's more like a place rehearsing being used. Practicing, endlessly, for people who never arrive. Which would mean it was never occupied at all. I can't prove that. I want to be clear, on the record, that I can't prove that."
Does: Says "rehearsing" quietly, almost to herself, testing the word. The two "can't prove that" lines come out faster, over-insistent, a person talking herself down. She exhales through her nose and sits motionless until static.

## Tape 3 — Office floor — "Mapping the Rules" (60s)

*Performance: compulsive. Her control has turned into ritual. She touches
the maps the way other people check locks.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "Recording nine. Monday the seventeenth. I've started mapping. Rooms, junctions, distances, how long the elevator takes to arrive when I call it. If this place has rules, and I believe it does, they'll show up in the numbers."
Does: The table is covered in hand-drawn maps, too many, edges aligned with obsessive neatness. While speaking she straightens one map that was already straight.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "The elevator is the one reliable thing down here. It comes when it's called. It goes where it goes. It has never once frightened me, and I can't say that about anything else here. I've timed it forty times. Forty consistent results."
Does: Warms up on "reliable," the only affection in the tape. Turns a map toward the lens, then pulls it back mid-gesture, hugging it against her chest for a moment before laying it down, as if showing it were dangerous.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "The problem is behind me. I walk a corridor, I draw it, I walk back the same way, and the drawing is wrong. Not badly wrong. Never badly wrong. A door short. A turn earlier than I recorded it. Small, deniable errors."
Does: Speaks to the maps, not the camera. On "behind me" she does not turn around, but her shoulders rise, a person deciding not to look.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "So either this place edits itself when I'm not looking, or I'm the instrument that's failing, and I can't tell you which of those frightens me more. I keep mapping anyway. The maps are wrong, but stopping feels worse."
Does: Finally looks up at the lens on "frightens me more" and smiles, a thin wrong smile that has nothing behind it. Gathers the maps into a pile and holds them like something rescued.

## Tape 4 — Airport floor — "Distance and Time" (60s)

*Performance: running on fumes, but fighting to sound precise. The horror is
watching precision cost her visible effort.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "Recording... I've stopped numbering them. It's the twentieth. Or the twenty-second. My watch says one thing, my notes say another, and I trusted both of them. I've trusted both of them my whole working life."
Does: Sits heavier, a dressing on one hand. Rubs her wrist over the watch, then keeps rubbing after the sentence ends, past the point where it means anything.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "Here is what I can actually state. The camera says I last recorded nineteen hours ago. My body says it's been days. I've eaten four times since then. Slept twice, I think. Those numbers don't belong in the same nineteen hours."
Does: Counts the points off on her fingers, deliberate, clinging to the method. On "slept twice, I think" she loses the count, looks at her own fingers a beat too long, and lets the hand drop.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "And I remember walking much further than nineteen hours holds. Hours of corridor I can still picture. I thought the elevators were carrying me down through distance, through floors of something real. I no longer think distance is what they move through."
Does: Her eyes de-focus on "hours of corridor," seeing it instead of the camera. She comes back to the lens on the last sentence, opens her mouth to continue, and stops herself with a small physical flinch, as if the next thought shouldn't be recorded.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "I have no evidence for what they move through instead. None. I want that on the record before I say anything else. It's a feeling, that's all it is, and lately my feelings have started arriving before the facts do."
Does: Folds her hands to deliver it formally, a witness giving testimony. On the final line her voice drops almost to a murmur and the formality collapses; she is telling the camera a secret.

## Tape 5 — School floor — "Contaminated Memories" (60s)

*Performance: the first personally frightened tape. Quiet throughout. She
handles her own memories like evidence from a crime scene.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "I found a room today that I remembered. Not recognized. Remembered. The scratch on the third desk, shaped like a fish hook. The coat hooks by the door, one bent upward. I knew it before I saw it."
Does: No greeting, no date, no preamble; she is mid-thought when the tape starts. Speaks quietly with an odd tenderness on the details, as if describing something beloved, which is the wrong emotion and she doesn't notice.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "I could have drawn that room before I opened the door, down to the light through the window. And I grew up six hundred miles from anywhere that room could possibly have existed. I checked my notes twice to be sure."
Does: Touches the closed notebook on "I checked my notes" and leaves the hand there, pressing down slightly, holding the facts shut.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "My childhood was somewhere else. That is a fact about me, and I had to check it. Sit with that for a moment, because I have. So either this place is reading what's in my head and building from it..."
Does: On "sit with that for a moment" she actually stops and sits with it, three full seconds of silence, staring at the lens without blinking. The pause should be uncomfortable to watch.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "...or it's writing things in, and my memories are the construction site. I don't know which. I can't know which. That's what makes it work. I used to know which of my memories were mine. That sentence should not be past tense."
Does: Hands folded so tightly the knuckles pale. On the last line her voice almost breaks, and instead of letting it she goes flat and quiet, which is worse. She does not look away.

## Tape 6 — Prison floor — "The Figures" (60s)

*Performance: a formal statement given by someone holding guilt at arm's
length. Level voice throughout; the effort of keeping it level should show.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "This one is practical. If you're following me, stop the tape when it ends and watch it again. There are others down here. You've probably seen one by now. If you haven't yet, you will."
Does: Sits straighter than in any recent tape, composed for the occasion. Delivers "you will" gently, like bad news to a patient, then lets a beat of silence sit.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "I won't describe them, because I'm not sure my description would be accurate, and inaccurate descriptions get people hurt. What I have verified: distance buys time. Doorways buy time. Neither one buys safety. They don't lose interest the way anything living would."
Does: Hands flat on the table, grounding herself. On "the way anything living would" her eyes drop to her own hands, and she takes an audible breath before going on.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "Light. The light from my phone. They won't come into it. They stand at the edge of it and wait. But hold it on them long enough and they don't retreat. They end. I've watched it happen. I've made it happen."
Does: Places her phone on the table, screen down, with exaggerated care, the way you'd set down a weapon. "I've made it happen" comes out quieter than everything around it, a confession rather than a finding.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "Earlier, I believed something else about them. Something reasonable. I recorded it as fact, the way I'm recording now. I was wrong, and I wasn't the one who paid for it. Check your light before you need it. Not after."
Does: A long pause before "I was wrong," jaw tight, eyes wet but nothing falls, and she doesn't blink it away. The final instruction is delivered straight into the lens with sudden force, the only hard edge in the tape.

## Tape 7 — Asylum floor — "Failed Explanations" (60s)

*Performance: hollowed-out lucidity. A scientist conducting the autopsy of
her own reasoning. One brief laugh in the wrong place.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "I've kept a list of explanations. Written down, in order. I'm reading it out loud so I can stop carrying it. A neurological event. An afterlife. A real structure, somewhere physical. A dream. Somewhere else entirely, with its own rules."
Does: Reads from a single creased sheet held in both hands. Reads the list in a flat ceremonial rhythm, like names at a memorial, a beat of silence between each item.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "Every one of them fit the evidence for a while. Every single one. That's the problem. The moment I adopted a theory, this place started producing evidence for it. Generously. Abundantly. Like it was agreeing with me, and enjoying it."
Does: Sets the paper down. On "enjoying it" a short laugh escapes her, one syllable, and she presses her lips together immediately after, frightened by her own laugh.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "So either I only notice what confirms me, which makes me a bad instrument. A compromised one. Or this place answers belief with proof, which is worse. Because then every answer I find down here was grown for me. To order."
Does: Absolute stillness, eyes locked on the lens, voice low and even. On "grown for me" her gaze drifts up and to the right, somewhere above the camera, and holds there through "to order" before returning.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "So I've stopped explaining. Not stopped observing. I still count, still measure, still record. But I've stopped explaining. There's a difference between those two things, and I think that difference is what has kept me alive this long."
Does: Slides the paper away from herself slowly, fingertips only, retiring it. The last line is calm, almost serene, and she nods once at the end, agreeing with herself, a private gesture the viewer was not meant to see.

## Tape 8 — Poolrooms floor — "The Bleed" (60s)

*Performance: detached calm that no longer belongs to a frightened person,
which is the frightening part. She is damp and never once acknowledges it.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "No date. I've let the dates go, and letting them go was easier than keeping them ever was. Something new to report. I hear the next place before I reach it now. Days before. It comes through early."
Does: Hair and collar visibly damp under the fluorescent light. Speaks slowly, unhurried, a strange peace on her. On "it comes through early" she tilts her head a few degrees, as if hearing something now, and does not comment on it.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "This time it was water. Dripping first, somewhere behind the walls. Then moving water. Then the smell of it, in rooms that were bone dry when I crossed them. By the time the elevator came, the water was already true."
Does: Recites the progression with her eyes half closed, remembering it in order, almost fond. A drop of water runs from her hairline down her temple during the line; she does not react to it at all.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "I used to think of these as separate places, stacked one above another, and the elevator as the seam between them. Now I think the seams are failing. Or there were never any seams at all. One place, wearing different rooms."
Does: Illustrates "stacked" with one flat hand over the other, then lets both hands fall on "never any seams." Says "wearing different rooms" directly to the lens, softly, watching the viewer take it in.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "I can't prove any of it. I notice I keep saying that, tape after tape. I can't prove that. It used to bother me more than it does now, and I suppose that should worry me too. It doesn't."
Does: A faint smile builds through the segment and arrives fully on "it doesn't." It is not reassurance and not madness, just distance. She holds the smile and the lens through the final silence.

## Tape 9 — Annex floor — "The Recordings" (60s)

*Performance: intimate and disoriented. She has stopped performing for a
future audience and started talking to one specific person she cannot see.
This is the tape where the viewer should feel watched back.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "I found tapes today. A whole drawer of them. My voice. My room, this room, this table. I have no memory of making them. That alone I could file under exhaustion. I've filed worse under exhaustion lately."
Does: A single unlabeled VHS tape lies in front of her. On "this room, this table" she touches the table with her fingertips, verifying it exists. The line about exhaustion comes with a tired half-smile that dies before it forms.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "But one of them says things I haven't said yet. Things I was planning to say tonight, in this recording, in words I hadn't chosen yet. I checked the tape twice before I pressed record. It's sitting right here."
Does: Rests two fingers on the tape without picking it up, keeping it pinned. Her breathing is audible in the pauses. On "it's sitting right here" she looks down at it as if it might have moved.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "So I'll talk to you instead. Whoever is behind me. Whoever is sitting where I can't see, watching this the way I watched hers. Mine. You're the only theory I haven't managed to break yet."
Does: Shifts her gaze slightly past the lens, to where the viewer's face would be, and keeps it there. The word "hers" comes out naturally; the correction to "mine" comes after a pause, and she visibly decides not to examine the mistake.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "I don't know if I'm leaving you warnings, or repeating warnings that were left for me, word for word, down to this sentence. I no longer think the difference matters to anyone but me. Maybe it never mattered at all."
Does: Still speaking past the lens, softer with each sentence, ending near a whisper. Then her lips keep moving through one final sentence the audio does not carry. She waits, as if for an answer, until static.

## Tape 10 — Data Center floor — "Acceptance" (60s)

*Performance: serene. Every earlier tape had tension in it somewhere; this
one has none, and the absence should unsettle. She has decided something and
is at peace with it, and the viewer never learns what.*

**0:00-0:15**
Says: "I'm not going to offer you an explanation. I don't have one. I've stopped grieving that, which took longer than you'd think. This is the last recording I intend to make, so I'll keep it plain."
Does: The table is bare for the first time, nothing in front of her, hands at rest. She looks better than in tapes 7 through 9, composed and clear-eyed, which should read as wrong rather than as recovery.

**0:15-0:30**
Says: "If you're here, then you've reached the place everything else was trying to become. I don't say that to frighten you. I say it because you've earned a plain sentence, and that's the plainest one I have."
Does: Absolute stillness, eyes steady on the lens, voice warm for the first time in the whole series. The warmth, arriving now, after everything, is the creepy note; play it with genuine kindness.

**0:30-0:45**
Says: "I believe I'm near an ending. I won't call it an escape. Those are different words, and down here the difference between them is everything. One more thing, and then I'm done, and the rest is yours."
Does: Straightens slightly in the chair, unhurried. On "the rest is yours" she opens her hands on the table, palms up, a small handing-over gesture, then lets them rest.

**0:45-0:60**
Says: "If what comes next looks familiar... if it looks like somewhere you know, somewhere you've missed... familiar is not the same as safe. Don't let that be the last mistake you make on my account. Go carefully. Go now."
Does: Leans in a fraction on "somewhere you've missed," the only urgency in the tape. Holds the lens a full two seconds past comfortable, expression unreadable, then reaches toward the VCR without saying goodbye.
