# It wants you to stay — Current Narrative Bible

**Status:** Authoritative current story direction  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-04

This document exists to prevent the story, tapes, and ending from drifting as
the game changes. It draws inspiration from the original *Narrative & Story
Specification v0.1*, but reflects the fixed floor order and current creative
decisions.

When another document, old concept, or proposed script conflicts with this
one, use this document for narrative work. Do not silently turn speculation
into confirmed lore. Amend this document deliberately when the story changes.

---

## Non-negotiable presentation

- Dr. Evelyn Cross is the only visible speaker and only recurring character.
- Every Dr. Cross recording uses the same environment.
- Every Cross recording is a restrained monologue directly to a fixed camera.
- The camera, framing, fluorescent lighting, and basic room remain consistent.
- There are no cutaways, inserts, flashbacks, reenactments, surveillance clips,
  exterior shots, or scenes illustrating what Cross describes.
- VHS degradation, tracking faults, analog audio, silence, and Cross's changing
  condition carry the presentation.
- Horror comes from what Cross says, what she cannot remember, and what she
  refuses to claim—not from theatrical performance.
- Cross is intelligent, observant, tired, and controlled. She never becomes a
  screaming prophet or an all-knowing lore dispenser.

The narrative structure contains:

1. One intro recording.
2. Ten mandatory elevator-room recordings, one for each non-final floor.
3. One player-centered outro sequence after the apparent escape.

The intro and ten elevator tapes feature Cross in the fixed recording setup.
The outro is not a Cross recording, contains no explanatory monologue, and is
experienced from the player's first-person perspective.

Bloom/Upside Down contains no recording. After ten floors of guidance, Cross's
absence in the final realm is intentional.

---

## Mystery contract

The game must never definitively explain:

- What the liminal realms are.
- Where they came from.
- Why the elevators exist.
- Who, if anyone, built the elevators.
- Why the charging stations exist.
- Why the player's phone works as it does.
- What the shadow figures are.
- Whether time operates normally.
- Whether Cross's memories are true.
- Whether Cross is alive when a recording is watched.
- Whether the player genuinely escapes.
- Whether the outside world changed, was replaced, or was never real.

Cross may form theories. Her theories may change or contradict one another.
None is confirmed as the solution.

The following rejected directions are not current canon:

- A confirmed corporate experiment or named secret project.
- A confirmed parasite using the player as an anchor.
- Cross definitively knowing the origin or purpose of the realms.
- A master villain, conspiracy, or final explanatory reveal.
- The tapes providing an objective scientific answer.

## Current gameplay context

Descent has eleven floors in a fixed order:

1. Casino
2. Mall
3. Office
4. Airport
5. School
6. Prison
7. Asylum
8. Poolrooms
9. Annex
10. Monolith
11. Bloom/Upside Down

The first ten floors end in an elevator objective room containing a television,
VCR, mandatory Cross recording, charging station, and elevator. Bloom ends in
the exit and has no tape.

The world seed changes the generated building and routes, but not this story
order. The Bleed quietly introduces elements of the next realm as the player
approaches the elevator.

The elevators are the one consistently trustworthy structure. They arrive,
they do not contain enemies, and they always advance the player. Their origin
and purpose remain unexplained.

The shadow figures are never named in dialogue. Their exact nature is unknown.
Current gameplay allows them to pursue through multiple doorways, and the
phone's light can hold them off and burn them away. Cross can describe her
observations but must not explain why these rules exist.

## Story spine

Cross entered the casino before the player and travelled through the same
sequence. She records herself because she initially believes careful evidence
will help her understand the place or help whoever follows her.

The factual story remains deliberately small:

- Cross investigates disappearances associated with an old Vegas casino.
- She misses a scheduled check-in.
- The player enters the casino looking for her or investigating her absence.
- The player becomes trapped after entering the forgotten elevator.
- Cross's recordings reveal how her confidence and memory deteriorated during
  her own descent.
- The player reaches an apparent exit from Bloom/Upside Down.
- What waits beyond it makes escape feel no better than imprisonment and offers
  no possibility that the player's life will ever improve.

The relationship between Cross and the player is intentionally unspecified for
now. Do not invent a familial, romantic, professional, or conspiratorial
connection without first making it a deliberate story decision.

The story progresses through Cross rather than through lore:

> observation → pattern → uncertainty → recognition → fear → failed theories →
> memory collapse → direct address → acceptance → the player's hopeless escape

## Visual and performance progression

The room and composition stay fixed. Progress is visible through Cross.

### Early recordings

- Clean appearance.
- Upright posture.
- Complete dates and clinical introductions.
- Organized notes.
- Confidence that evidence will produce an explanation.

### Middle recordings

- Fatigue and small injuries.
- Longer pauses before factual claims.
- Inconsistent dates.
- Corrections made mid-sentence.
- The first admission that her own memories may be unreliable.

### Late recordings

- Emotionally exhausted but never hysterical.
- Less interest in proving anything.
- Direct acknowledgment of a future viewer.
- Uncertainty over whether she has made the recording before.
- Calmness that feels closer to surrender than relief.

The room may exhibit extremely subtle continuity uncertainty, but it remains
recognizably the same set. Do not use conspicuous background spectacle to carry
the story.

## Intro recording — “If I Miss My Check-In”

Cross is composed and speaking from the established recording room.

She explains that she is investigating disappearances associated with an aging
Las Vegas casino. She expects to be gone only several hours and has arranged
for the message to reach the player if she misses a scheduled check-in.

She mentions an unmarked service elevator found in a forgotten part of the
casino. She does not know where it goes. She does not ask to be rescued.

Her final instruction is sincere:

> “If I haven't contacted you by morning, do not come looking for me.”

The recording ends before Cross explains what she intends to do next. The
player subsequently enters the casino and becomes trapped.

## The ten elevator recordings

### Tape 1 — Casino — “The First Elevator”

Cross describes entering the unmarked elevator and emerging somewhere that
could not physically exist beneath the casino. She remains methodical and
believes a rational explanation is likely. She has begun recording evidence
for herself and for anyone who might follow.

**Emotional state:** Controlled, curious, confident.  
**Story movement:** Establishes that Cross made the same descent before the
player.

### Tape 2 — Mall — “Places Without People”

Cross observes that empty places continue performing their functions. Lights
cycle, announcements play, and machines wait to be used. She wonders whether
these environments were abandoned or whether they were never genuinely
occupied.

**Emotional state:** Scientific fascination with an emerging unease.  
**Story movement:** The spaces may imitate purpose without understanding it.

### Tape 3 — Office — “Mapping the Rules”

Cross has begun mapping rooms, timing elevators, and documenting repeatable
behavior. The elevator appears reliable, but her maps become inaccurate behind
her. She believes the place has rules while refusing to guess whether those
rules are natural, designed, or learned.

**Emotional state:** Focused, increasingly compulsive.  
**Story movement:** Introduces the conflict between reliable observations and
an environment that will not remain stable.

### Tape 4 — Airport — “Distance and Time”

Her watch, recorded timestamps, memory, hunger, and exhaustion no longer agree.
She remembers travelling far longer than the camera records. She speculates
that the elevators may not move through physical distance, then immediately
admits that she has no evidence for what they do instead.

**Emotional state:** Measured uncertainty.  
**Story movement:** Time and chronology become unreliable. This is the first
recording in which Cross cannot confidently state the date.

### Tape 5 — School — “Contaminated Memories”

Cross describes recognizing a classroom and remembering specific details from
childhood. She then acknowledges that she attended school somewhere entirely
different. She cannot decide whether the place is drawing from her memories or
placing memories inside her.

**Emotional state:** Personally disturbed for the first time.  
**Story movement:** The threat moves from architecture into Cross's identity.

### Tape 6 — Prison — “The Figures”

Cross calmly reports what she has learned about the shadow figures without
naming them. Distance and doorways can buy time but do not end pursuit
immediately. The phone's light appears to repel or destroy them. She says an
earlier assumption was wrong and that someone else paid for her mistake, but
does not explain who.

**Emotional state:** Restrained guilt.  
**Story movement:** Establishes that Cross's observations are fallible and that
the rules carry consequences.

### Tape 7 — Asylum — “Failed Explanations”

Cross lists theories she once considered—neurological event, afterlife,
physical structure, dream, another world—and explains why she no longer trusts
any of them. She worries that the act of forming a theory makes her notice only
the evidence that supports it.

**Emotional state:** Exhausted, lucid, losing faith in reason as protection.  
**Story movement:** Explicitly rejects an explanatory lore reveal.

### Tape 8 — Poolrooms — “The Bleed”

Cross describes encountering sounds, moisture, objects, and architecture from
the next place before reaching its elevator. She wonders whether the realms
are connected, overlapping, or slowly becoming one place. Her clothes or hair
may be damp, but she never acknowledges it.

**Emotional state:** Quietly frightened, no longer surprised.  
**Story movement:** The separate environments appear to be converging.

### Tape 9 — Annex — “The Recordings”

Cross has found evidence that she made recordings she cannot remember making.
Some contain statements she believes she has not said yet. She begins speaking
to “whoever is behind me.” She cannot determine whether she is leaving warnings
or repeating warnings that were left for her.

**Emotional state:** Intimate, disoriented, directly aware of the viewer.  
**Story movement:** The tapes themselves become part of the mystery without
being explained as a mechanism.

### Tape 10 — Monolith — “Acceptance”

Cross is exhausted but almost peaceful. She no longer offers theories. She
believes she is near an ending, but refuses to call it an escape. The sentiment
from the original narrative now belongs here:

> “If you're here, then you've reached the place everything else was trying to
> become.”

She warns that if the next place looks familiar, familiarity must not be
mistaken for safety. After a long pause, she reaches toward the VCR.

**Emotional state:** Calm surrender.  
**Story movement:** Removes guidance immediately before Bloom and prepares the
player for an ending that cannot be trusted.

## Bloom/Upside Down — silence

There is no Cross recording in Bloom.

The player must finish the last realm without explanation, instruction, or the
comfort of Cross's voice. The silence is a narrative event, not missing
content.

---

## Outro sequence — “Outside”

The outro belongs entirely to the player.

There is no Dr. Cross recording, no narrator, no final explanation, and no
human voice telling the player how to interpret what they see. The sequence
remains first-person. The player is never shown.

After surviving Bloom/Upside Down, the player passes through the apparent exit.
For a brief moment, the transition should feel like success. The light, open
space, or absence of the enclosing architecture creates the instinctive relief
of finally being outside.

That relief must collapse completely.

What lies beyond is not home, rescue, or a subtly imperfect return to ordinary
life. It is an exterior state of absolute desolation: vast, dead, silent, and
without any credible route back to the human world. The precise imagery can be
designed later, but its emotional reference is the ending of *The Beyond*—an
apparent escape that reveals only a larger and more final form of damnation.

The player may have escaped the rooms. Nothing has become better. There is no
reason to believe it ever will.

### Required emotional beats

1. **Release:** The player crosses the final threshold and experiences space,
   light, or distance after prolonged confinement.
2. **Recognition:** The environment cannot be the world they were trying to
   return to.
3. **Search:** The player looks or moves for some sign of life, shelter, a road,
   a landmark, or a way back.
4. **Finality:** Nothing answers. The exit is gone, useless, or leads only back
   into the same hopeless condition.
5. **Dread:** The scale of the outside makes the earlier interiors seem almost
   merciful.

The ending should communicate, without text or dialogue:

> You got out. Nothing got better for you. Nothing ever will.

Do not soften the ending with a survivor, a distant city, a functioning road,
a hopeful light, a rescue signal, or evidence that the player can eventually
return home. Mystery remains, but hope does not.

The final image should hold long enough for the player to understand that this
is not another obstacle to solve. It is where they are now.

## Dialogue rules for every script

Each recording should contain:

1. One concrete observation.
2. One implication or theory.
3. One reason Cross doubts that implication.
4. One small change in her emotional state or memory.
5. No definitive answer.

Cross should naturally use phrases such as:

- “I don't know.”
- “I thought…”
- “I was wrong.”
- “I can't prove that.”
- “I remember it differently.”
- “I've stopped trying to understand.”

Avoid:

- Lore monologues that explain the setting.
- Proper nouns for the phenomenon or the figures.
- Technical jargon used to disguise exposition.
- Villain speeches.
- Sudden hysteria.
- Prophecy presented as fact.
- Confirming that Cross's latest theory is correct.
- Showing any event she can describe.
- Dialogue that literally announces the current floor's theme.

Cross's story is the evidence of what the place does to a person. The mystery
surrounding her must remain larger than the facts she can provide.

---

## Deliberately open decisions

These points are not canon yet:

- Cross's exact relationship to the player.
- How the intro recording reaches the player.
- The exact visual form of the dead exterior in the player-centered outro.
- Whether the “someone else” mentioned in Tape 6 was real.
- Whether subtle changes to the recording room are continuity errors, memory
  errors, or evidence of the realms.
- The exact length and final dialogue of every recording.

Resolve these only through an explicit story decision. Do not fill them in by
accident while writing scripts or implementing playback.
